Act one — before EatOps

Before.

A café that works hard, and still loses the day.

06:40 Opening

The morning starts with a guess.

Nobody's quite sure what sold out yesterday. The board gets wiped and rewritten from memory. Two items on it ran out at four in the afternoon and nobody wrote it down.

→ The day begins with information that's already wrong.
11:20 Rush

The rush eats the system.

Orders on paper, shouted across the pass. Table six gets table four's order. Somebody's oat flat white gets made twice and paid for once. Nobody has time to fix any of it until closing.

→ Every busy hour costs money nobody can trace.
15:05 Stockout

You find out you're out — from a customer.

The oat milk ran out an hour ago. So did the cold brew. The message asking someone to reorder is sitting in a group chat with four hundred unread messages, under a photo of somebody's dog.

→ Stock is only real once a guest is disappointed.
22:15 Close

The day gets reconciled by hand.

Cash counted twice. A spike of paper tickets. A spreadsheet that hasn't been honest since March. The numbers are off by a few hundred rupees and nobody will ever know why.

→ You close the café, but you never really close the day.
The turn

Nothing about the café changes.
Except that it can finally see itself.

Same room. Same machine. Same two people opening at six. EatOps doesn't replace any of it — it connects it, so every part of the café knows what every other part is doing.

Act two — on EatOps

After.

The same café, the same day, finally running itself.

06:40 Opening

The morning starts with a number.

Last night's close already told you what sold, what's running low and what to prep. The menu updated itself the moment the last item ran out. Nothing on the board is a guess.

→ You open knowing exactly where you stand.
11:20 Rush

The rush runs itself.

Guests scan and order from the table. Tickets land in the kitchen the second they're placed, with the table number attached. Table six gets table six's order. Every time, without anyone shouting.

→ The busiest hour is now the calmest part of the system.
15:05 Stocked

You knew about the oat milk on Tuesday.

Stock counts down with every order that uses it. The alert reached you with three days of cover still left — not three hours after a guest asked for something you couldn't make.

→ You reorder on a schedule, not in a panic.
22:15 Closed

The day closes itself.

Sales, stock movement, loyalty points and staff hours are already reconciled. There's one number to read. You read it, lock the door, and actually go home.

→ The day ends when you leave, not three hours later.
The difference, plainly

Same café. Same day. Two very different ledgers.

Nothing here is theoretical — it's the difference between a café that runs on memory and one that runs on a system.

What's included

Six modules. One connected café.

Every part talks to every other part — that connection is the whole point. Built custom for your café, not configured from a template.

01 — Front of house

Digital Menu & Ordering

A QR menu with photos, descriptions and live prices. Guests scan, browse and order from their phone — no app to download, nothing to reprint.

02 — Kitchen

Live Orders

Every table and takeaway order appears in the kitchen the moment it's placed, with the table number attached. No missed tickets, no dishes going to the wrong table.

03 — Guests

Loyalty Program

Points, tiers and birthday perks that run on their own. Guests earn, redeem and come back — no punch cards, nothing tracked by hand.

04 — Back of house

Inventory

Stock counts down with every order that uses it. You get alerted with days of cover remaining — long before anything actually runs out.

05 — Team

Team Communication

A dedicated channel for your staff — stock alerts, shift changes, maintenance requests. Not buried in the same chat as weekend plans.

06 — Equipment

Asset Tracking

Every piece of equipment logged — from the espresso machine to the POS terminal. Know what you own, where it is, and when it's due for service.
